-- territory: nearest stadium wins
SELECT ST_Intersection(v.geom, e.geom)
FROM (SELECT (ST_Dump(ST_VoronoiPolygons(
  ST_Collect(geom)))).geom FROM stadiums) v, england e;

-- three nearest rivals (KNN)
SELECT name, ST_Distance(geom::geography,
  :me::geography)/1000 AS km
FROM stadiums ORDER BY geom <-> :me LIMIT 4;

-- reach rings
SELECT ST_Buffer(geom::geography, 100000)
FROM stadiums WHERE club = :me;

-- clubs within 100 km
SELECT count(*) FROM stadiums
WHERE ST_DWithin(geom::geography,
  :me::geography, 100000);

Where did each gameweek’s travel “happen”? The average position of the ten matches, weighted by distance, so long trips pull the centre. It moves by tens of kilometres because the league is compact. An unweighted average never moves at all: every club plays once a week, so it always equals the league centre.

Where is loyalty up for grabs? The bright bands are ground that lies within a few kilometres of the border between two territories. Pick how wide the band is. This measures geography, not fandom, because clubs also draw fans from far away.

Each dot is a completed match: how far the away side travelled against its goal difference. A small sample and a simple model, so treat it as a conversation starter, not a verdict.

How it is calculated

Distances

Straight-line distance over the Earth's surface between stadium coordinates. In PostGIS the same number comes from ST_Distance on the geography type.

Territories

Every point in England belongs to its nearest stadium: a Voronoi diagram clipped to a hand-traced outline of England. In PostGIS: ST_VoronoiPolygons.

Sun and light

The sun's position comes from a solar algorithm accurate to about half a degree, with UK clock changes. "Floodlights" means the sun is below the horizon at kick-off.

Fixtures and results

Limits

Stadium coordinates and the England outline are approximate. The distance and results chart uses a small sample and shows correlation, not cause.
